## 1. First screen rule

On a 375px phone the first screen shows four things and nothing else that
competes: the outcome, a hint of the mechanism, one CTA, one credibility
signal that is true and documented. Value proposition readable in under 10
seconds. 57 percent of viewing time happens above the fold and 74 percent in
the first two screens (NN/g 2018). The fold does not kill scrolling, a weak
first screen does.

## 2. Direct-response lander anatomy (paid traffic to an offer or lead form)

1. Headline: specific outcome plus mechanism tease. Same vocabulary as the ad.
2. Subhead: who it is for and what happens next, under 30 words.
3. Primary CTA, 48px tall, full width on mobile, benefit label.
4. Proof strip: three to five items, each documented (counts, credentials,
   media, guarantee). No fabricated numbers. No implied doctor or celebrity.
5. Mechanism section: what is different, told as discovery, not features.
6. Testimonials: signed, with a typical-results line in the same view when
   the result is atypical.
7. Offer and guarantee, stated plainly. No countdown, no fake stock.
8. Repeated CTA, identical to the first.
9. FAQ, five to seven questions that remove objections.
10. Final CTA, identical.
11. Disclosure block adjacent to any claim it qualifies (see section 6).
12. Footer: privacy, terms, contact, affiliate disclosure where applicable.

Copy rhythm: paragraphs 2 to 3 sentences, grade 6 readability, plain verbs.
One primary CTA style. Persistent CTA for warm or lower-price traffic.

## 3. VSL page

- Headline above the player, first thing read, promises the outcome and
  hints the mechanism without giving it away.
- Player with a custom thumbnail, a visible play button, captions on by
  default because many watch muted first.
- Under the player: one line of credibility, then the CTA behavior chosen
  by traffic temperature. Cold high-ticket: CTA appears at the reveal
  point. Warm or lower price: persistent CTA visible from the start.
- Below: the lander anatomy from section 2, items 4 to 12, for readers who
  do not watch.
- Never autoplay with sound. Never hide the disclosure until after the
  video.

## 4. Bridge page (between the ad and the vendor VSL)

Three paragraphs (Palmer framework):

1. Discovery: a named credible source introduces a novel root cause. Visceral
   language, felt not explained.
2. Proof: specific people, specific numbers, specific places. Only what is
   documented.
3. CTA: restate the problem, urgency about the information not the product,
   direct them to the video.

Design: one column, 680px reading width, 18px body, headline 32 to 40px,
one image or none, CTA button after paragraph 3 and again at the end,
disclosure block at the top or immediately under the headline on affiliate
pages. Message match with the ad is a design requirement: same mechanism
name, same tone, same promise.

## 5. Brand site anatomy (agency, personal, partner brand)

- Hero: the most characteristic thing about the subject. Split or
  asymmetric layout. Portrait or product on one side, statement on the
  other. Kicker, headline, lede, one CTA, one quiet secondary link.
- Proof: numbers with tabular figures and short labels, real logos on a
  neutral tile, one line stating that every claim has paperwork.
- Story: 2 to 4 sentences at body-lg, one accent phrase.
- Offer or portfolio: a list with rules or a two-column zig-zag, never an
  equal three-card grid. Each item has a name, one-line hook, one line of
  substance, one link.
- Doors (ways to work together): headline per door, who it is for, three
  bullets, one action. Fees in a small table with tabular numerals.
- Human section: lists with rules, italic for notes to self.
- Contact: a qualifying form with three questions, or a single email.
- Footer: location, legal, socials, sentence case.

One memorable move per page. On a dark editorial site that is usually the
display type. On a light site it is usually one color moment.

## 6. Components

CTA button
- Height 48px on landers, 44px minimum elsewhere. Full width under 640px.
- Label is the outcome: "Watch the free video", "Get the 3 questions", "Book
  the 15 minutes". Never "Submit", "Continue", "Learn more".
- One primary style per page, one ghost style for secondary actions.
- Active state moves 1px down, hover raises contrast, focus ring visible.
- Same label and style at top, middle, bottom.

Lead form
- 3 fields or fewer on paid traffic. Label above, placeholder shows the
  pattern and ends with an ellipsis.
- `type="email"`, `type="tel"` with `inputmode="tel"` and a mask,
  `autocomplete` on every field.
- Required and optional both marked. Errors inline next to the field, first
  error focused on submit, message says what to do.
- Submit enabled until the request starts, then spinner inside the button.
- Never block paste. Confirmation replaces the form in place.

Disclosure block (required on any page with health, income, or results claims)
- Placement: adjacent to the claim it qualifies, in the same viewport,
  never behind a link or a scroll.
- Size: 12pt (16px) or larger on ClickBank pages, never smaller than body
  caption elsewhere. Same contrast as body text, never dimmed below 4.5:1.
- Content: what the claim does not mean, typical results, material
  connection or affiliate relationship, FDA statement where a supplement
  is involved.
- Never contradicted by a headline, badge, or image on the same page.

Testimonial
- Name, location, photo only with signed release. Quote in body size, not
  caption size. Atypical result gets a typical-results line inside the
  same card. No before and after photos of body parts.

Proof strip
- Three to five items. Number in tabular figures at title size, label in
  label size. Every number has a source in the repo or in Ben's records.
- Logos on a neutral tile at uniform optical height. No fabricated
  partner badges.

## 7. Conversion research behind the rules

- Attention: 57 percent above the fold, 74 percent in two screens. Users
  leave in 10 to 20 seconds without a clear value proposition (NN/g).
- Concise, scannable, objective copy improved measured usability 124
  percent (NN/g).
- Benchmarks: median lander 6.6 percent, health and wellness 5.1 percent,
  lead-gen 9 to 12 percent with top quartile 20 to 30 (Unbounce, 41k pages).
- Forms: average checkout 11.3 fields, 8 recommended, only 14 percent mark
  required and optional, 64 percent skip phone masking (Baymard).
- Google M3 Expressive research, 46 studies: bolder, larger, higher-contrast
  primary actions were found up to four times faster and closed the age gap
  in find time. Directly relevant to Boomer audiences.
- Speed: 53 percent leave past 3 seconds on mobile. Each second can cost
  up to 20 percent of retail conversion (Google).
- FTC: disclosures must be unavoidable, clear, adjacent, and consistent
  with the rest of the ad. If an effective disclosure is not possible, the
  claim is not made.
